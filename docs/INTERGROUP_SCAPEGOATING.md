# Intergroup Scapegoating: Creating Political Boundaries

## Discovery

**Major Finding**: Scapegoating an outgroup member **unifies the ingroup** through shared hostility, creating a clear us-them political boundary. This models **Schmittian friend-enemy distinction** computationally.

## The Mechanism

### Starting Conditions
- **Community A**: Internally cohesive (all positive edges)
- **Community B**: Internally cohesive (all positive edges)
- **Inter-community**: Sparse connections (positive, negative, or none)

### Event: A Member Accuses B Member
```
A0 (Community A) accuses B0 (Community B)
```

### Information Contagion Process

1. **A0 → B0**: Initial accusation (edge flips to negative)
2. **A0 → A1**: Information spreads through Community A
3. **A1 → B0**: A1 creates negative edge (heard from A0)
4. **A1 → A2**: Continues spreading within A
5. **A2 → B0**: A2 creates negative edge
6. **etc.**

### Result

**Community A (Ingroup)**:
- All members develop negative edge to B0
- Internal positive edges maintained/strengthened
- **UNIFIED through shared enemy**

**Community B (Outgroup)**:
- Never hears about accusation (disconnected)
- Internal cohesion intact
- **UNAWARE** of being targeted

## Empirical Results

### Test 1: Basic Outgroup Scapegoating

**Setup**: Two communities, minimal connection

**Results**:
- Community A: **4/4** turned against B0 (100% unification)
- Community B: **0/3** heard about it (0% awareness)
- Inter-group edges: All became negative to B0

**Conclusion**: ✓ Complete ingroup unification against outgroup member

### Test 2: Pre-existing Antagonism

**Setup**: Communities already have negative edges

**Results**:
- Scapegoating **amplifies** existing hostility
- Converts partial antagonism to unified antagonism
- All of Community A unified against B member

**Conclusion**: ✓ Strengthens mutual antagonism

### Test 3: Destroying Cooperation

**Setup**: Communities with positive inter-group connections

**Results**:
- Positive edges: 2 → 1 (cooperation destroyed)
- Creates **polarization** where cooperation existed
- Converts ambivalence to hostility

**Conclusion**: ✓ Scapegoating destroys inter-group cooperation

## Theoretical Framework

### Schmittian Friend-Enemy Distinction

**Carl Schmitt's Thesis**: "The political" is defined by the friend-enemy distinction.

**Our Computational Model Shows**:
1. Scapegoating **CREATES** the distinction
2. Groups define themselves **AGAINST** an outgroup
3. Identity emerges through **opposition**
4. The "political" is generated by **scapegoating dynamics**

### Formal Statement

For Community A accusing member S from Community B:

**Ingroup Unification**:
```
∀ a ∈ A: edge(a, S) → -1
```
All ingroup members develop hostility to outgroup scapegoat

**Boundary Creation**:
```
∃ clear division: A (unified) vs S (enemy)
```
Sharp us-them distinction emerges

**Asymmetric Awareness**:
```
Awareness(A, S) = True
Awareness(B, scapegoating) = False
```
One-sided polarization (until/unless B learns)

## Comparison: Intragroup vs Intergroup Scapegoating

| Aspect | Intragroup | Intergroup |
|--------|------------|------------|
| **Target** | Internal member | External member |
| **Mechanism** | Expulsion/isolation | Boundary creation |
| **Result** | All-against-one | Us-against-them |
| **Goal** | Purify community | Define community |
| **Awareness** | Target is aware | Target may be unaware |
| **Structure** | Scapegoat isolated | Scapegoat stays in their group |
| **Function** | Restore internal unity | Create external identity |

### Intragroup (Classical Girardian)
```
Before: [A, B, C, D, E]
After:  [A, B, C, D] vs [E]

E is expelled/isolated from the community
```

### Intergroup (Political/Schmittian)
```
Before: [A, B, C] and [D, E, F]
After:  [A, B, C] UNIFIED vs D (from other group)

D remains in their group
A, B, C achieve cohesion through shared enemy
```

## Political Implications

### 1. External Enemies Unify Internal Factions

**Mechanism**: Scapegoating outgroup member creates ingroup cohesion

**Applications**:
- "Rally around the flag" effect
- Wartime national unity
- Political scapegoating of foreigners
- Xenophobia as unification tool

**Quote**: "Nothing unites people like a common enemy"

### 2. Identity Formation Through Opposition

**Mechanism**: Group defines itself by who it opposes

**Process**:
1. Accuse outgroup member
2. Spread accusation through ingroup
3. Ingroup unifies in hostility
4. **Identity emerges**: "We are those who oppose them"

**Examples**:
- Nationalism (us vs other nations)
- Partisanship (us vs other party)
- Religious conflict (us vs heretics)
- Ethnic conflict (us vs them)

### 3. Polarization Dynamics

**Starting**: Mixed/ambivalent relations

**Process**: Scapegoating converts ambivalence to hostility

**Ending**: Clear division, no middle ground

**Danger**: Destroys cooperation, creates conflict

### 4. Escalation Risk

**One-sided phase**: Community A unified against B member, B unaware

**Escalation trigger**: Community B learns about hostility

**Result**:
- B unifies **AGAINST** Community A
- Symmetric polarization
- Conflict escalation
- **Us vs them** becomes mutual

### 5. Political Utility of Scapegoating

**For leaders**: Scapegoating outgroups is politically useful
- Unifies fractious ingroup
- Creates clear identity
- Distracts from internal problems
- Mobilizes population

**Quote**: "Politics is the art of creating enemies" (variation of Schmitt)

## Girard Extended: Two Types of Scapegoating

### Type 1: Intragroup (Original Girardian)

**Purpose**: Restore internal unity by expelling a member

**Mechanism**:
- Community in crisis
- All turn against one member
- Member is expelled/killed
- Unity restored

**Structure**: Universal scapegoating (everyone against scapegoat)

**Function**: Purification ritual, social healing

### Type 2: Intergroup (Political Extension)

**Purpose**: Create external identity through shared enemy

**Mechanism**:
- Group accuses outgroup member
- Ingroup unifies in hostility
- Boundary created
- Identity formed

**Structure**: Partial scapegoating (one group against outgroup)

**Function**: Political identity formation, group cohesion

## Information Contagion Perspective

### Why Ingroup Unifies

**BFS propagation through ingroup**:
1. Accuser tells friends in Community A
2. They tell their friends in Community A
3. Information spreads through positive (friendship) edges
4. Everyone in A eventually hears
5. All create negative edge to outgroup scapegoat

**Why Outgroup Doesn't Hear**:
- No information path from A to B
- Disconnected communities
- Contagion is LOCAL to accuser's community

### Requirement for Symmetric Escalation

For Community B to learn and respond:
- **Bridge must exist**: Some A-B connection
- **Information crosses**: Someone tells B about accusation
- **B's contagion**: Information spreads through B
- **B unifies**: Against A or specific A member

**Result**: Symmetric polarization, conflict

## Mathematical Formalization

### Ingroup Cohesion Score

```
InCohesion(A) = (Positive internal edges) / (Max possible internal edges)
```

**Before**: High (community already cohesive)
**After**: High or higher (maintained/strengthened)

### Outgroup Hostility Score

```
OutHostility(A, S) = |{a ∈ A : edge(a, S) = -1}| / |A|
```

**Before**: 0 or low (few hostile)
**After**: 1.0 (all hostile)

### Polarization Metric

```
Polarization(A, B) = |OutHostility(A, B) - OutHostility(B, A)|
```

**Symmetric**: 0 (both groups equally hostile)
**Asymmetric**: >0 (one-sided polarization)

**After intergroup scapegoating**: Asymmetric (A hostile, B unaware)

## Historical and Contemporary Examples

### Classic Pattern
- **Foreign scapegoats** during national crisis
- **Minority groups** blamed for societal problems
- **Political opponents** scapegoated during elections
- **"The other"** in wartime propaganda

### Why It Works
1. **Unifies ingroup** (creates cohesion)
2. **Clear identity** (we oppose them)
3. **Distracts** from internal divisions
4. **Mobilizes** population toward action

### Danger
- Creates **real enemies** where none existed
- **Self-fulfilling prophecy** (they respond in kind)
- **Escalation spiral** (mutual scapegoating)
- **Destroys cooperation** and peace

## Computational Predictions

### When Intergroup Scapegoating Succeeds

**Requirements**:
1. **Cohesive ingroup**: Connected positive edges in A
2. **Sparse inter-group ties**: Few A-B connections
3. **Active propagation**: Accusation spreads through A
4. **No back-channel**: B doesn't learn about it

**Result**: A unifies, B unaware, asymmetric polarization

### When It Escalates to Conflict

**Triggers**:
1. **B learns** about scapegoating
2. **Bridge exists** for information to flow
3. **B's leaders** spread counter-narrative
4. **B unifies** against A

**Result**: Symmetric polarization, mutual hostility, conflict

### When It Fails

**Failures**:
1. **A is fragmented**: Accusation doesn't spread
2. **A-B are integrated**: Too many positive cross-ties
3. **B member is bridge**: Scapegoating isolates A
4. **Internal backlash**: A members reject accusation

## Implications for Conflict Resolution

### Prevention Strategies

1. **Increase cross-ties**: More positive A-B connections
2. **Integrate communities**: Make separation costly
3. **Early detection**: Identify scapegoating attempts
4. **Counter-narratives**: Spread alternative stories

### De-escalation After Polarization

1. **Break information isolation**: Connect communities
2. **Humanize outgroup**: Personal relationships
3. **Third-party mediation**: External perspective
4. **Shared superordinate goals**: Common enemies/goals

### Structural Resilience

**Resilient to intergroup scapegoating**:
- Highly integrated communities (many cross-ties)
- Weak ingroup boundaries (unclear us-them)
- Distributed leadership (no central propagators)
- Cross-cutting cleavages (multiple group identities)

## Conclusion

**Main Finding**: Intergroup scapegoating creates political identity by unifying ingroup against outgroup through shared hostility.

**Schmitt Validated**: The computational model shows how the "friend-enemy distinction" emerges from scapegoating dynamics.

**Girard Extended**: Two mechanisms:
1. **Intragroup**: Expel member, restore unity (purification)
2. **Intergroup**: Target outgroup, create boundary (identity)

**Political Reality**: External enemies are **politically useful** for creating internal cohesion, making outgroup scapegoating a recurring pattern in history.

**Danger**: Creates real enemies, triggers escalation, destroys cooperation.

**Key Insight**: "Us vs them" is not given - it's **manufactured** through scapegoating.
